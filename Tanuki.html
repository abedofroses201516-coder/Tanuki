Reading Passage

One day, a traveling salesman walked along a dusty road near a small village. He carried many things to sell, like cups, bowls, cloth, and tools. But he felt tired and sad because no one wanted to buy his goods. The villagers had already seen his things before. He walked to the edge of the village near the forest, hoping to find new customers.

Suddenly, he heard a strange sound. It came from the forest. He stopped and listened carefully. He walked into the trees and found a tanuki. The tanuki looked like a raccoon. It was stuck between branches. It looked scared and weak. The salesman felt sorry for the poor animal. He gently moved the branches and freed the tanuki. After it was free, the tanuki ran away into the forest.

The next day, the salesman walked his usual road again. He saw an old kettle on the ground. It was rusty, dirty, and ugly. He decided to clean it. That night, he washed it and rubbed it with a cloth until it became shiny. The next morning, he took the kettle to a temple. The monks were serious, and they bought the kettle for a tea ceremony.

During the ceremony, something strange happened. The kettle became cold very fast, so they had to heat it again and again. When it was hot, it began to move. At the end of the ceremony, the monks were unhappy and said the kettle was not normal. The next morning, they put the kettle on the fire. Suddenly, it began to change. It grew a tail, soft paws, and a pointed nose. The salesman opened his eyes wide and recognized the tanuki he saved. The tanuki smiled and said it became a kettle to thank him, but being too hot hurt. Everyone laughed. From that day on, the tanuki stayed at the temple as a guest. People came from far away to see the kind tanuki, and the salesman visited often to drink tea from a normal kettle.
